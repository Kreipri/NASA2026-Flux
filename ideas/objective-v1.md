# Main Objective, reconciled (proposed, 2026-11-15)

**Problem (Bea):** three framings are mixed up. D-020: "prove this ridge can host a Moon base". Proposed: "Junior Astronaut training". Fact check: "7 days = first landings". A base with a habitat, shelter and arrays isn't a first landing, so what is the crew actually there for?

## Three things the objective must match
| Must match | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Hackathon brief** (`CONTEXT.md`) | Run an outpost. Balance life support, radiation, power. See choices play out. No free choice. Real data. |
| **Why NASA would put people there** (sourced) | 1. **Learn to live and work far from Earth, and test systems before Mars** (F04, Moon Base page). 2. **Study deep-space radiation:** the Moon is outside Earth's magnetic field (F28, new). 3. **Find and use ice:** water, air, fuel (F11, F12). |
| **A game for ages 7-12** | One clear goal, a reason to take risks (otherwise the best move is to hide all week), and honest endings. |

## Proposed objective (one sentence)
> **"Be the first crew to live in the new Moon base. Keep everyone safe for a week, and prove the base does what it was built for."**

- **Survive** (the brief's trade-offs): power, oxygen, dose, stamina. Crew safe = the base works.
- **The 4 tests = the real reasons NASA is there:**
  - **Light:** log the sunlight; does the power last? (living off Earth)
  - **Radiation:** measure it; the data matters for Mars (research)
  - **Shelter:** test it in a real storm (living off Earth)
  - **Ice:** survey the dark crater (resources + science)
- **Why 7 days:** we play the base's **first week**, the shakedown. NASA's base concept has crews staying about a month (F27). The first week is our story choice and is labelled `[✎ Story]`. We never claim "NASA plans 7-day stays".
- **Ending:** "Week 1 complete: the base kept its crew safe." Tiers stay (COMPLETE / HONORS / GOLD PATCH / NOT YET).

This keeps D-020's evidence idea ("prove the site") but moves it from the *ridge* to the *base*. That's easier for kids ("does our home work?") and truer to NASA's phased plan (build the base, then test it with crews).

## v1.1 (2026-11-15, Bea's answers)

**Wrapper decided (Bea):** in-world it's a **real mission** to the commander and crew. Only we (the players) know it's a game. We use fiction to **compress** real facts into one mission. Real old storms stay honest through their badge ("Real storm, May 10 2024 · ARCHIVE"), not through the story. Supersedes options A/B above.

**Ice purpose corrected (Bea is right):** the crew doesn't *use* ice. They **survey** it: find out where water is and whether future crews could live off it (`ice-science-check.md` option 1, D-020). NASA's reasons: (1) **science:** clues to how the Moon and solar system formed; Artemis objective "understanding the character and origin of lunar polar volatiles"; (2) **prospecting:** location, form and amount, before anyone can rely on it; (3) only later, **using** it for water, air and fuel. Kid line: "Find out where water is hiding, and whether future crews could use it to live here." (F11, F29)

**7 weeks instead of 7 days (proposed, recommended):** 49 days sits inside NASA's base concept range (~28 days → ~60 days, F27). It's more accurate and needs no "first week" excuse.
- **Structure:** 1 turn = 1 week (still 7 turns, ~15 min). Kids plan their crew's week like a school week. **Alert days zoom in:** when a storm, eclipse or quake hits mid-week, the game pauses for a decision ("Shelter now?"). That's where the drama goes.
- **Accuracy gains:** a full lunar day (~29.5 days) fits, so lighting changes and long shadows are real. The Earth blackout can last up to ~2 weeks, about half of each month, twice per mission (F19, F20). Several storms from the deck can appear.
- **Costs and risks:** the engine rescales from per-day to per-week numbers; the timeline and sim must be redone; storms become interrupts rather than whole turns. "Week" units are more abstract for 7-year-olds, which the alert days offset.
- Fact card: "NASA's base plans have crews of up to 4 staying about 1-2 months. Your mission: 7 weeks." (F27)

**What crews actually do (sourced, for the weekly job list):**
- ISS routine: day about 06:00-21:30 GMT, **2.5 h exercise a day**, science and maintenance, daily conferences with Mission Control, ~8 h sleep, lighter weekends with family calls. (F60, F61)
- Moon surface (planned): moonwalks for geology, **collecting samples, deploying instruments**, observing the site, a plant-growth experiment (LEAF), and crew health data. (F62)
- Apollo 17 (longest): 75 h on the surface, 3 moonwalks totalling ~22 h. (F02)
