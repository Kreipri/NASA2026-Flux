**Date:** 2026-11-15. **Status:** concept draft; no art or code built.

**Earlier exploration:** `character-concepts-v3.md` is the current cast draft (Curio helper AI, separate field rover). V2 retains research on rover backstories, animal-spaceflight Easter eggs, and professions versus crew assignments. CADRE and the cast below were never approved.

## Direction from Bea
- The commander is human and represents the child/player.
- The other astronauts may be animals: cute and friendly is the aim, species not chosen.
- Base Bot should connect to a robot NASA actually engineered, possibly a rover/drone adaptation.
- Commander customization is a possible future feature, time permitting.
- Keep the existing four-person crew: commander + three specialists. The robot is equipment/a companion, not a fifth astronaut or extra daily crew time.

## Bot: three directions

| Direction | Real NASA reference | Character treatment | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Little scout rover — recommended** | NASA/JPL CADRE: small solar-powered, four-wheeled rovers with stereo cameras, designed to cooperate | Compact chassis, visible wheels and solar panel; cameras suggest eyes. A small head tilt and changing camera aim carry expression. Friendly, observant, slightly literal | Strong small-companion silhouette; CADRE's actual experiment is a rover team, not a talking solo south-pole assistant |
| **B. Polar explorer** | VIPER: lunar polar resource-exploration rover design with headlights and a drill | Sturdy body, camera mast, headlights, small sampling tool. Patient and determined; loves investigating a dark patch | Closest connection to ice and shadows; bulkier silhouette, and its field-science role could compete with the geologist |
| **C. Indoor floating helper** | Astrobee: NASA's fan-propelled robots for work inside the ISS | Softened cube, camera face, small perching arm. Organizes equipment and explains the console | Easy to place beside dialogue; the real robot depends on air and microgravity, so it cannot simply float around the lunar exterior or habitat |

**Recommendation:** start with A. Working name **Pip**, entirely provisional. Warm-white body, navy camera housing, one amber status light, four dark wheels, visible solar panel. Avoid a generic giant face screen: let useful robot parts give it character. Original proportions and artwork; keep the engineering reference recognizable.

There are two honest ways to frame the NASA connection:
- A **stylized real CADRE rover**, with a fictional nickname, voice, and role in our training story. Best fit if the character itself must be a NASA-engineered robot. Explain the real rover team in its source card.
- An **original CADRE-inspired companion**, with a new body/capabilities. More design freedom; credit it as inspired by NASA/JPL, rather than presenting our invented model as actual NASA hardware.

No identity choice between those treatments is locked. Do not imply a real CADRE or VIPER mission took place at our south-pole base. No mission launch dates or flight outcomes are needed for this concept.

Pip handles the tour, explains readings, and offers the existing kid-question chips. It gives information; the commander makes the call. A sample line: "That shadow is moving toward our panels. Want to check tomorrow's power?" Its humor comes from its personality, not incorrect science. Any physical rover job still uses the existing action costs; a talking mascot does not grant free work or free samples.

## First animal crew proposal

All names below are placeholders. These are astronauts with engineering, medical, or geology backgrounds, retaining the existing three gameplay voices; species do not add abilities or meters.

| Character | Visual hook | Personality and useful tension | Sample line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commander — the player** | Human, same suit family as the crew, simple commander patch | Player-owned identity: no fixed backstory or scripted personality. Others trust the commander's choices | Crew address the player as "Commander" |
| **Tavi — beaver, engineering background** | Broad muzzle, small rounded ears, rust-brown fur, little tool pouch | Inventive and encouraging; gets absorbed in improvements and needs reminders that repairs use time | "I can fix that panel. Who will check the air while I work?" |
| **Remy — rabbit, medical background** | Long ears contained inside an adapted helmet, pale-grey fur, compact medical kit | Calm and decisive; cares about safety while understanding that the mission needs progress | "Let's get everyone under cover. The sample can wait." |
| **Mica — mole, geology background** | Small round ears, charcoal fur, pink nose, sample case | Delights in tiny discoveries; sometimes wants one more measurement before coming home | "One sample tells us about one spot. Can we make time for another?" |

**Why this lineup:** distinct faces and helmet silhouettes, with a clear connection between each character and their work. **Trade-off:** animal/job pairings are familiar and could feel predictable. Give each a second trait unrelated to species; avoid making the rabbit timid, the beaver always right about repairs, or the mole able to see in lunar darkness. If the mole's face loses readability at portrait size, a red panda is a possible geologist alternative, still unchosen.

The shared mission provides enough explanation for humans and animals working together. Suggested brief story label: "A make-believe crew. Real space science." No animal-origin subplot is needed.

## Visual and writing rules
- Friendly, capable colleagues: warm expressions, readable shapes, restrained proportions. Test that ages 10-12 do not read them as preschool characters.
- Shared suit shapes and patch placement make one team. Species features stay inside sealed, adapted helmets on the surface; no exposed ears or tails.
- Match D-022/D-023: clean flat shapes in portraits; simple forms and two-tone shading in gameplay. Fur, faces, and silhouettes must distinguish characters without relying on suit color alone.
- The commander gets equal visual care. A simple placeholder human portrait is enough now; refer to them as "you"/"Commander" so later customization fits the writing.
- Keep later customization cosmetic: skin tone, hair, name, suit accent. No gameplay bonus and no avatar-builder requirement for the prototype.
- Crew requests expose competing needs without guilt. Every specialist can support a sensible decision to wait; no one always supplies the correct answer.
- Fictional animal physiology does not change the existing shared air, stamina, or dose rules. Animal facts and species-specific survival mechanics are outside this draft.
- NASA engineering facts belong in source cards; the voice, nickname, animal crew, and fictional deployment are story. Register any bot facts in `fact-sources.md` before displaying them in the app.

## Verified engineering references

Checked 2026-11-15 against official pages; facts describe the designs, not a claim of completed missions.
- [NASA/JPL — CADRE](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/cadre/): four wheels, solar power, stereo cameras, small rover team; developed by JPL.
- [NASA Science — VIPER In Depth](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viper/in-depth/): polar resource prospecting, headlights, drilling, and light/shadow route constraints.
- [NASA — Astrobee free-flying space robots](https://www.nasa.gov/technology/robotics/nasa-seeks-input-for-astrobee-free-flying-space-robots/): electric fans, cameras, interchangeable arms, routine tasks inside the station. Its real operating environment does not transfer to the lunar surface.
