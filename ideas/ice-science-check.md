# Lunar ice: fact check and prototype recommendation

2026-11-14. User requested real-life accuracy and challenged why the crew collects ice. Research only; no engine changes.

## Verified distinctions

- **Science:** lunar water and other volatiles offer evidence about the Moon and solar-system history. Samples and measurements help investigate their origin and distribution. NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-science/ and https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/
- **Resource prospecting:** determine location, form, concentration and distribution before deciding whether a deposit is usable. NASA explicitly identifies remaining knowledge gaps: https://www.nasa.gov/overview-in-situ-resource-utilization/
- **Resource production:** excavating/drilling, extraction, processing, transport and storage are separate engineering tasks. Water can supply drinking water or be split to produce oxygen and hydrogen; that requires equipment and energy. NASA's current technology overview describes collection, processing, storage and use: https://www.nasa.gov/lunar-surface-technology/ . NASA-hosted extraction architecture includes cleanup, electrolysis and storage: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230008775/downloads/NIAC_2020_PhI_Metzger_AquaFactorem.pdf

## Correction to the prototype explanation

The permanent air-power discount per sample has no support in these sources. Having water feedstock does not by itself make oxygen machinery permanently more energy-efficient. This is a design inference from the documented processing chain, not a quoted NASA finding. A finite collected quantity could yield a finite oxygen quantity after processing, not unlimited future discounts.

The game's 30% find chance and scouting bonuses are game tuning, not validated probabilities for the mission site. A percentage of wet terrain in another crater does not validate a per-trip discovery probability. Any retained odds must be SIMULATED and not presented as NASA-derived predictions.

## Options

1. **Research and site survey (recommended):** scout -> collect soil/ice-bearing samples -> analyze -> record evidence about where water exists and whether it is accessible. Costs crew time, power and exposure; earns report-card evidence, no survival discount. Best fit for a short trainer and existing report-card goal. Less direct survival payoff. A dry sample is still evidence; quality/coverage should matter alongside discoveries so luck alone does not set success.
2. **Small extraction experiment:** sample -> powered extraction test -> measured, finite water/oxygen yield. Demonstrates a plausible future system; label equipment as a fictional mission assumption and yields as SIMULATED. Clear practical payoff, but adds processing rules and must not imply an operational lunar supply chain already exists.
3. **Production outpost:** collect bulk material -> extract/purify water -> split water -> store oxygen, spending power and consuming feedstock. Strong resource loop; much larger scope and an explicitly future infrastructure scenario.

Suggested child-facing objective: “Find out where water is hiding and whether future crews could use it.”

Recommendation proposes revising D-013's immediate resource benefit; no option selected or code changed in this check.
